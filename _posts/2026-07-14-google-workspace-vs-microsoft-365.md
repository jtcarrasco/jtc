Every small business eventually has to pick a side: Google or Microsoft. Both give you business email, documents, file storage and video calls. Both cost about the same. And both will serve you just fine, which, weirdly, makes picking one harder than it should be.

Here's a practical breakdown.

## The short version

**Pick Google Workspace if:** you're starting from scratch, you already use Gmail personally, or your team works on the same documents together a lot.

**Pick Microsoft 365 if:** your clients or your industry live in Word and Excel, you already use Outlook, or you need the full desktop versions of the Office apps.

## What you get with each

At the roughly $12 to $13 per user per month level, both plans include:

| | Google Workspace | Microsoft 365 |
|---|---|---|
| Email | Gmail (custom domain) | Outlook (custom domain) |
| Documents | Docs, Sheets, Slides | Word, Excel, PowerPoint |
| Storage | 2TB pooled | 1TB per user |
| Video calls | Google Meet | Microsoft Teams |
| Calendar | Google Calendar | Outlook Calendar |
| Desktop apps | Web only | Yes (Word, Excel, etc.) |
| Built-in AI | Google Gemini | Microsoft Copilot |

The big practical difference: Microsoft 365 comes with the full desktop versions of Word and Excel. Google's apps run in your web browser (they do have an offline mode that works reasonably well).

## Where Google wins

Google really is better when people work on the same document together. Several people can edit the same Google Doc at the same time and it just works, with no "which version is the latest?" headaches. If you have employees or contractors who share documents, that's a real advantage.

Google is also easier to set up if this is your first business account. The admin settings are simpler, and if you already live in Gmail, there's almost nothing new to learn.

**Google Gemini** is now built into Workspace. It can summarize email threads, help draft replies in Gmail, write content in Docs, create formulas in Sheets and transcribe Google Meet calls. It's included in most paid Workspace plans.

## Where Microsoft wins

If your clients send you Word documents, Excel spreadsheets or PowerPoint files, they'll open more reliably in Microsoft's own apps than in Google's. The formatting usually holds up in Google, but "usually" isn't "always," and with client-facing documents, that gap matters.

Microsoft also wins if you work in accounting, law, healthcare or any field where Excel is the main tool. Google Sheets is good, but it doesn't do everything Excel does, and switching can be painful if your work is built around Excel-only features.

**Microsoft Copilot** is built into 365. It works a lot like Gemini: drafting emails in Outlook, summarizing meetings in Teams and writing content in Word and Excel. If your team already lives in Microsoft's world, Copilot fits right in.

## The AI factor

Both platforms have AI built in now, and for small businesses that's more and more the reason to pick one over the other. If you want your team actually using AI, the easiest path is whichever platform they already live in. No new apps, no switching tabs. They just ask Gemini or Copilot from inside the tools they use all day.

## My recommendation by type of business

**Realtors, consultants and service businesses:** Google Workspace. You're probably not maintaining giant spreadsheets, and the teamwork tools and easy setup will serve you better.

**Accountants, bookkeepers and finance:** Microsoft 365. Your clients live in Excel. Don't fight it.

**Restaurants, retail and local services:** either one works. Google is easier to start with.

**Teams with remote workers:** Google Workspace, for the teamwork tools.

## One more thing: you can switch later

Neither choice is forever. Moving email and files from one to the other is annoying, but doable. Don't let the decision freeze you. Pick the one that fits best today and get back to running your business.
