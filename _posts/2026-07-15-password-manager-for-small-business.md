Most small business data breaches don't start with some movie-style hacker in a hoodie. They start with a password.

Usually it's a password that got reused on a bunch of accounts, written on a sticky note, or just too weak to survive a basic guessing attack. If your business email, QuickBooks and Stripe all share the same password, one breach opens all three doors.

The fix is a password manager. It takes about 30 minutes to set up, and it solves most of this problem for you.

## What a password manager does

A password manager keeps all your passwords in an encrypted vault. You remember one master password, and the app handles the rest. It creates strong, unique passwords for every account, fills them in for you, and keeps them in sync between your phone and your computer.

The result: every account gets its own random 20-character password, and the only one you have to remember is the one that opens the vault.

## Which one to use

For most small businesses, one of these three will do the job:

**[1Password](https://1password.com)** (plans start around $3 per user per month) is the easiest to use and has solid business features. It's a good pick if you have employees and need to share logins with your team.

**[Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com)** has a generous free plan and is open source, which means independent security researchers have looked under the hood. If it's just you, the free version is all you need.

**Apple Passwords** (built into iPhone and Mac since iOS 18) works fine if everyone in your business uses Apple devices. It's now its own app with more features than the old Keychain, including shared password vaults for families or small teams. The price is right (free), but it's limited once you step outside the Apple world.

## Getting started

Setup works about the same way for all of them:

1. Create an account and install the browser extension and the phone app.
2. Start with your most important accounts: business email, banking, accounting software and anywhere you take payments.
3. For each one, let the password manager create a new, strong password and save it.
4. Turn on two-factor authentication anywhere it's offered (it's a separate layer of protection, more on that below).

You don't have to move everything over at once. Start with the accounts that would hurt the most if someone broke into them, then work through the rest over a few weeks.

## Two-factor authentication

While you're at it, turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) for your business email. With 2FA, even if someone gets your password, they also need a code from your phone to log in. It's the single most effective thing you can do to protect your email, and it takes about five minutes.

Gmail, Microsoft 365 and most big services support it. Look for it in your account's security settings.

## A quick word on passkeys

You might start seeing "passkey" as a login option on Google, Apple and other sites. A passkey replaces your password with a secure key stored on your device, and you log in with Face ID or your fingerprint instead. Passkeys are more secure than passwords and much harder to phish. Your password manager handles them too, so there's nothing special you need to do.

---

## The 30-minute version

- Download Bitwarden (free) or 1Password (free trial)
- Install the browser extension
- Save your top 5 accounts with new, generated passwords
- Turn on 2FA for your business email

That's it. You've just done more for your security than most small businesses ever do. Go ahead and throw away the sticky note.
